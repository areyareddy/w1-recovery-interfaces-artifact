### Proof

**Problem Analysis:**
We are given three positive real numbers \( x, y, z \) with the following equations:
1. \( x + \frac{1}{y} = 4 \),
2. \( y + \frac{1}{z} = 1 \),
3. \( z + \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7}{3} \).

We need to prove that \( x y z = 1 \).

**Approach:**
To solve this, we can express \( \frac{1}{y} \), \( \frac{1}{z} \), and \( \frac{1}{x} \) in terms of \( x, y, z \) using the given equations and then multiply them together to find a relationship that simplifies to \( x y z = 1 \).

**Step 1: Express the reciprocals in terms of \( x, y, z \):**
From the first equation:
\[ \frac{1}{y} = 4 - x. \]
From the second equation:
\[ \frac{1}{z} = 1 - y. \]
From the third equation:
\[ \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7}{3} - z. \]

**Step 2: Multiply the reciprocals:**
Multiply the three expressions:
\[ \left( \frac{1}{y} \right) \left( \frac{1}{z} \right) \left( \frac{1}{x} \right) = (4 - x)(1 - y)\left( \frac{7}{3} - z \right). \]
Simplify the left-hand side:
\[ \frac{1}{x y z} = (4 - x)(1 - y)\left( \frac{7}{3} - z \right). \]

**Step 3: Expand the right-hand side:**
First, multiply \( (4 - x)(1 - y) \):
\[ (4 - x)(1 - y) = 4 - 4 y - x + x y. \]
Then multiply by \( \left( \frac{7}{3} - z \right) \):
\[ (4 - 4 y - x + x y)\left( \frac{7}{3} - z \right) = \frac{28}{3} - 4 z - \frac{28}{3} y + 4 y z - \frac{7}{3} x + x z + \frac{7}{3} x y - x y z. \]
This expansion is quite complicated, but we can find a better approach by substituting back the original variables.

**Alternative Approach:**
Instead of expanding directly, we can substitute the expressions for \( x, y, z \) in terms of each other using the given equations.

From \( \frac{1}{y} = 4 - x \), we get:
\[ y = \frac{1}{4 - x}. \]

From \( \frac{1}{z} = 1 - y \), we get:
\[ z = \frac{1}{1 - y} = \frac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{4 - x}} = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x}. \]

From \( \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7}{3} - z \), we get:
\[ x = \frac{1}{\frac{7}{3} - z} = \frac{3}{7 - 3 z}. \]

Now, substitute \( z = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} \) into the expression for \( x \):
\[ x = \frac{3}{7 - 3 \cdot \frac{4 - x}{3 - x}} = \frac{3}{7 - \frac{12 - 3 x}{3 - x}} = \frac{3}{\frac{21 - 7 x - 12 + 3 x}{3 - x}} = \frac{3}{\frac{9 - 4 x}{3 - x}} = \frac{3 (3 - x)}{9 - 4 x}. \]

Thus, we have:
\[ x = \frac{3 (3 - x)}{9 - 4 x}. \]

Multiply both sides by \( 9 - 4 x \):
\[ x (9 - 4 x) = 3 (3 - x). \]
Expand:
\[ 9 x - 4 x^2 = 9 - 3 x. \]
Rearrange:
\[ -4 x^2 + 12 x - 9 = 0 \]
or equivalently:
\[ 4 x^2 - 12 x + 9 = 0. \]

This is a quadratic equation in \( x \):
\[ 4 x^2 - 12 x + 9 = 0. \]
The discriminant is:
\[ \Delta = (-12)^2 - 4 \cdot 4 \cdot 9 = 144 - 144 = 0. \]
Thus, there is a unique real solution:
\[ x = \frac{12}{8} = \frac{3}{2}. \]

Now, substitute \( x = \frac{3}{2} \) back to find \( y \) and \( z \):
\[ y = \frac{1}{4 - x} = \frac{1}{4 - \frac{3}{2}} = \frac{1}{\frac{5}{2}} = \frac{2}{5}. \]
\[ z = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} = \frac{4 - \frac{3}{2}}{3 - \frac{3}{2}} = \frac{\frac{5}{2}}{\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{5}{3}. \]

Finally, compute \( x y z \):
\[ x y z = \left( \frac{3}{2} \right) \left( \frac{2}{5} \right) \left( \frac{5}{3} \right) = 1. \]

However, we need to ensure that all steps are valid, especially that the denominators are not zero and that the substitutions are correct.

**Verification of Assumptions:**
1. \( 4 - x \neq 0 \): Since \( x = \frac{3}{2} < 4 \), this is true.
2. \( 1 - y \neq 0 \): Since \( y = \frac{2}{5} \neq 1 \), this is true.
3. \( \frac{7}{3} - z \neq 0 \): Since \( z = \frac{5}{3} \neq \frac{7}{3} \), this is true.
4. \( 3 - x \neq 0 \): Since \( x = \frac{3}{2} \neq 3 \), this is true.

Thus, all substitutions are valid and the solution is correct.

**Conclusion:**
The unique solution to the system is \( x = \frac{3}{2} \), \( y = \frac{2}{5} \), \( z = \frac{5}{3} \), and we have \( x y z = 1 \).

However, the problem is more general: it asks to prove that \( x y z = 1 \) under the given conditions, without necessarily finding the exact values of \( x, y, z \). The above method finds the exact values, but we can also derive \( x y z = 1 \) directly from the original equations without solving for \( x, y, z \) explicitly. Here is a more direct approach:

**Direct Proof:**
Multiply all three given equations:
\[ \left( x + \frac{1}{y} \right) \left( y + \frac{1}{z} \right) \left( z + \frac{1}{x} \right) = 4 \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{7}{3} = \frac{28}{3}. \]

Expand the left-hand side:
\[ \left( x y + \frac{x}{z} + \frac{y}{y} + \frac{1}{y z} \right) \left( z + \frac{1}{x} \right) = \left( x y + \frac{x}{z} + 1 + \frac{1}{y z} \right) \left( z + \frac{1}{x} \right). \]
\[ = x y z + \frac{x y}{x} + \frac{x}{z} z + \frac{x}{z x} + z + \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y z} z + \frac{1}{y z x} \]
\[ = x y z + y + x + \frac{1}{z} + z + \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{x y z}. \]

Thus:
\[ x y z + y + x + \frac{1}{z} + z + \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{x y z} = \frac{28}{3}. \]

But from the original equations:
\[ x + \frac{1}{y} = 4, \quad y + \frac{1}{z} = 1, \quad z + \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7}{3}. \]
Add these three equations:
\[ x + y + z + \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{z} = 4 + 1 + \frac{7}{3} = \frac{28}{3}. \]

Substitute this into the expanded product:
\[ x y z + \left( x + y + z + \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{z} \right) + \frac{1}{x y z} = \frac{28}{3} + x y z. \]
But \( x + y + z + \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{z} = \frac{28}{3} \), so:
\[ x y z + \frac{28}{3} + \frac{1}{x y z} = \frac{28}{3} + x y z. \]
Subtract \( \frac{28}{3} + x y z \) from both sides:
\[ \frac{1}{x y z} = 0. \]
But \( x, y, z > 0 \), so \( x y z > 0 \), and thus \( \frac{1}{x y z} > 0 \). This is a contradiction unless \( x y z = 1 \).

Wait, this seems incorrect because \( \frac{1}{x y z} = 0 \) is impossible. I must have made a mistake in the expansion or substitution.

**Identifying the Mistake:**
The mistake is in the expansion of the product. The correct expansion of \( (x + \frac{1}{y})(y + \frac{1}{z})(z + \frac{1}{x}) \) is:
\[ (x + \frac{1}{y})(y + \frac{1}{z})(z + \frac{1}{x}) = x y z + x \cdot \frac{1}{z} \cdot z + \frac{1}{y} \cdot y \cdot z + \frac{1}{y} \cdot \frac{1}{z} \cdot z + x y \cdot \frac{1}{x} + x y \cdot \frac{1}{x} \cdot \frac{1}{z} + \frac{1}{y} \cdot \frac{1}{z} \cdot \frac{1}{x} \cdot z + \frac{1}{y} \cdot \frac{1}{z} \cdot \frac{1}{x} \cdot \frac{1}{z}. \]

This is too messy, so perhaps a better approach is to use the expressions for the reciprocals:
\[ \frac{1}{y} = 4 - x, \quad \frac{1}{z} = 1 - y, \quad \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7}{3} - z. \]
Multiply them:
\[ \frac{1}{x y z} = (4 - x)(1 - y)\left( \frac{7}{3} - z \right). \]

Now, expand \( (4 - x)(1 - y) \):
\[ 4 - 4 y - x + x y. \]
Then multiply by \( \frac{7}{3} - z \):
\[ (4 - 4 y - x + x y)\left( \frac{7}{3} - z \right) = \frac{28}{3} - 4 z - \frac{28}{3} y + 4 y z - \frac{7}{3} x + x z + \frac{7}{3} x y - x y z. \]
Thus:
\[ \frac{1}{x y z} = \frac{28}{3} - 4 z - \frac{28}{3} y + 4 y z - \frac{7}{3} x + x z + \frac{7}{3} x y - x y z. \]

Now, multiply both sides by \( x y z \):
\[ 1 = \frac{28}{3} x y z - 4 x y z z - \frac{28}{3} x y y z + 4 x y z y z - \frac{7}{3} x x y z + x x y z z + \frac{7}{3} x x y z y - x x y z x y z. \]
This seems even more complicated. 

Perhaps we need another approach. Notice that the expressions for \( x, y, z \) are not independent. 

But we already found that \( x = \frac{3}{2} \), \( y = \frac{2}{5} \), \( z = \frac{5}{3} \) is a solution, and in this case \( x y z = 1 \). 

To avoid solving for \( x, y, z \) explicitly, we can instead compute \( x y z \) directly using the given equations. 

Here is a better approach:
From \( \frac{1}{y} = 4 - x \), we get \( y = \frac{1}{4 - x} \).
From \( \frac{1}{z} = 1 - y \), we get \( z = \frac{1}{1 - y} = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} \).
From \( \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7}{3} - z \), we get \( x = \frac{3}{7 - 3 z} = \frac{3}{7 - 3 \cdot \frac{4 - x}{3 - x}} = \frac{3 (3 - x)}{9 - 4 x} \), leading to \( x = \frac{3}{2} \) as before.

But we can also use the expressions to find \( x y z \):
\[ x y z = x \cdot \frac{1}{4 - x} \cdot \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} = \frac{x}{3 - x}. \]
But from the earlier equation \( x = \frac{3 (3 - x)}{9 - 4 x} \), we can solve for \( x \):
Multiply both sides by \( 9 - 4 x \):
\[ x (9 - 4 x) = 3 (3 - x), \]
\[ 9 x - 4 x^2 = 9 - 3 x, \]
\[ -4 x^2 + 12 x - 9 = 0, \]
\[ 4 x^2 - 12 x + 9 = 0. \]
The discriminant is \( 0 \), so \( x = \frac{12}{8} = \frac{3}{2} \).

Then:
\[ x y z = \frac{x}{3 - x} = \frac{\frac{3}{2}}{3 - \frac{3}{2}} = \frac{\frac{3}{2}}{\frac{3}{2}} = 1. \]

This is much simpler and avoids dealing with the messy expansion.

**Final Direct Proof:**
We have \( y = \frac{1}{4 - x} \), \( z = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} \), and \( x y z = \frac{x}{3 - x} \).

But from the third equation \( \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7}{3} - z \), we get:
\[ z = \frac{7}{3} - \frac{1}{x}. \]
But we also have \( z = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} \), so:
\[ \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} = \frac{7}{3} - \frac{1}{x}. \]
Multiply through by \( 3 x (3 - x) \):
\[ 3 x (4 - x) = 7 x (3 - x) - 3 (3 - x). \]
Expand:
\[ 12 x - 3 x^2 = 21 x - 7 x^2 - 9 + 3 x. \]
Simplify:
\[ 12 x - 3 x^2 = 24 x - 7 x^2 - 9. \]
Rearrange:
\[ 4 x^2 - 12 x + 9 = 0. \]
This is the same as before, and we get \( x = \frac{3}{2} \).

Then:
\[ x y z = \frac{x}{3 - x} = 1. \]

Thus, \( x y z = 1 \).

But we can also note that \( x y z = \frac{x}{3 - x} \), and from \( 4 x^2 - 12 x + 9 = 0 \), we have \( x = \frac{3}{2} \), so \( 3 - x = \frac{3}{2} \), and \( x y z = 1 \).

But we can also note that:
\[ 4 x^2 - 12 x + 9 = 0 \implies (2 x - 3)^2 = 0 \implies x = \frac{3}{2}. \]
Then \( y = \frac{1}{4 - x} = \frac{2}{5} \), \( z = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} = \frac{5}{3} \), and \( x y z = 1 \).

But to avoid solving for \( x, y, z \) explicitly, we can proceed as follows:

From \( y = \frac{1}{4 - x} \) and \( z = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} \), we have:
\[ x y z = x \cdot \frac{1}{4 - x} \cdot \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} = \frac{x}{3 - x}. \]

From the third equation, \( \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7}{3} - z \), we have \( z = \frac{7}{3} - \frac{1}{x} \). But we also have \( z = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} \), so:
\[ \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} = \frac{7}{3} - \frac{1}{x}. \]
Multiply through by \( 3 x (3 - x) \):
\[ 3 x (4 - x) = 7 x (3 - x) - 3 (3 - x). \]
Simplify:
\[ 12 x - 3 x^2 = 21 x - 7 x^2 - 9 + 3 x. \]
\[ 12 x - 3 x^2 = 24 x - 7 x^2 - 9. \]
\[ 4 x^2 - 12 x + 9 = 0. \]
\[ (2 x - 3)^2 = 0. \]
\[ x = \frac{3}{2}. \]

Then:
\[ x y z = \frac{x}{3 - x} = \frac{\frac{3}{2}}{\frac{3}{2}} = 1. \]

Thus, \( x y z = 1 \).

### Step-by-Step Abstract Plan

1. **Express \( y \) in terms of \( x \):**
   - From \( x + \frac{1}{y} = 4 \), we get \( y = \frac{1}{4 - x} \).

2. **Express \( z \) in terms of \( x \):**
   - From \( y + \frac{1}{z} = 1 \), we get \( z = \frac{1}{1 - y} = \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} \).

3. **Use \( x y z = \frac{x}{3 - x} \):**
   - Compute \( x y z = x \cdot y \cdot z = x \cdot \frac{1}{4 - x} \cdot \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} = \frac{x}{3 - x} \).

4. **Relate \( x \) and \( z \) using the third equation:**
   - From \( z + \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7}{3} \), we get \( z = \frac{7}{3} - \frac{1}{x} \).
   - Set this equal to \( \frac{4 - x}{3 - x} \) and solve for \( x \).

5. **Solve for \( x \):**
   - Multiply through by \( 3 x (3 - x) \) to get \( 3 x (4 - x) = 7 x (3 - x) - 3 (3 - x) \).
   - Simplify to get \( 4 x^2 - 12 x + 9 = 0 \), which gives \( x = \frac{3}{2} \).

6. **Compute \( x y z \):**
   - Substitute \( x = \frac{3}{2} \) to get \( x y z = \frac{x}{3 - x} = 1 \).
