### Proof and Analysis

First, we need to understand the statement:
\[ \prod_{k=0}^{5} (2k + 1) \mod 10 = 5 \]

This is equivalent to:
\[ (1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 9 \cdot 11) \mod 10 = 5 \]

#### Step 1: Compute the product modulo 10
We can compute the product modulo 10 by computing each factor modulo 10 and then multiplying them together modulo 10. 

First, we note that:
\[ 2k + 1 \equiv 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 \mod 10 \]
since \( k \) ranges from 0 to 5:
- \( 2 \cdot 0 + 1 = 1 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 2 + 1 = 5 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 7 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 4 + 1 = 9 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 5 + 1 = 11 \equiv 1 \mod 10 \)

But wait, \( 2 \cdot 5 + 1 = 11 \equiv 1 \mod 10 \) is incorrect. The correct sequence is:
- \( 2 \cdot 0 + 1 = 1 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 2 + 1 = 5 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 7 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 4 + 1 = 9 \)
- \( 2 \cdot 5 + 1 = 11 \equiv 1 \mod 10 \) (since \( 11 - 10 = 1 \))

Thus, the sequence is:
\[ 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 1 \mod 10 \]

#### Step 2: Multiply the residues modulo 10
Now, multiply the residues in order modulo 10:
1. \( 1 \cdot 3 = 3 \mod 10 \)
2. \( 3 \cdot 5 = 15 \equiv 5 \mod 10 \)
3. \( 5 \cdot 7 = 35 \equiv 5 \mod 10 \)
4. \( 5 \cdot 9 = 45 \equiv 5 \mod 10 \)
5. \( 5 \cdot 1 = 5 \mod 10 \)

Thus, the product is:
\[ 5 \mod 10 \]

This is the desired result.

   - Multiply the terms in order modulo 10.
   - The final result is \( 5 \mod 10 \).
